TUFLOW FV–SCHISM Hydrodynamic and Thermohaline Comparison

A six-day, like-for-like benchmark using an exact shared horizontal mesh, common timestamps, fixed physical sampling elevations and same-coordinate ocean residual diagnostics.

Prepared by Dongfang Liu and Badin Gibbes1–7 May 2011 AEST144 hourly recordsLower · Middle · Upper
Core result

1. Executive summary

The two models reproduce water-surface elevation closely, but they develop substantially different vertical thermohaline structures. The divergence is most pronounced in the middle and upper estuary, where TUFLOW FV develops a saline, warm lower layer while SCHISM remains comparatively fresh and cold through much of the water column.

Interpretation boundaryThis is an inter-model verification exercise, not validation against observations. The report identifies where and how the models diverge; it does not establish which model is more accurate.

2. Comparison framework and processing methodology

The comparison was designed to remove avoidable differences in horizontal geometry, time selection and sampling position. The remaining differences therefore primarily reflect model formulation, vertical discretisation, forcing implementation and transport/mixing behaviour.

1

Exact horizontal mesh match

Both models use 1,419 nodes and 1,375 elements: 215 triangles and 1,160 quadrilaterals. Coordinates and connectivity are identical. Bathymetry matches after sign reversal.

2

Fixed internal locations

Lower = cell/element 1359; Middle = 681; Upper = 113. All are internal elements and do not touch the prescribed ocean or river boundaries.

3

Common time window

The comparison uses 144 hourly records from 1 May 2011 01:00 to 7 May 2011 00:00 AEST. Only exact common timestamps are retained.

4

Horizontal interpolation

SCHISM nodal values are interpolated to each TUFLOW cell centre. The three selected elements are quadrilaterals, so four-node bilinear shape-function weights are used.

5

Common physical elevations

Surface = 0.5 m below the mean of the two model surfaces; mid-depth = midpoint between that reference surface and bed; near-bottom = 0.5 m above bed.

6

Vertical interpolation order

TUFLOW profiles are interpolated from layer centres. SCHISM is first interpolated vertically at each element node and then horizontally to the common cell centre.

Metrics and naming

All reported differences use SCHISM − TUFLOW FV. The raw TUFLOW variable W and raw SCHISM variable verticalVelocity are displayed as Vz and compared without sign reversal. Because vertical-velocity definitions may not be perfectly equivalent across the two model systems, Vz is treated as supporting rather than primary evidence.

Same-coordinate ocean diagnosticThe ocean residual analysis samples six TUFLOW cell centres adjacent to the ocean boundary and interpolates SCHISM to the identical coordinates. Segment-length weighting is then applied. This is not a direct face-integrated transport calculation.

3. Lower-estuary results

The lower location is the closest of the three points to the ocean boundary and is the deepest selected cell. Water level agrees very closely, while velocity components and thermohaline properties show clear differences in magnitude, vertical shear and tidal variability.

Water surface

Water-level RMSE is 0.0142 m, bias is +0.0082 m and correlation is 0.995. This is the strongest water-level agreement among the three locations.

Temperature and salinity

SCHISM exhibits stronger tidal-scale oscillation in temperature and salinity, whereas TUFLOW FV develops a more persistent vertical gradient. Salinity RMSE ranges from 8.27 psu at the surface to 10.31 psu at mid-depth; temperature RMSE is approximately 1.59–1.65 °C across the three levels.

Velocity

Vx and Vy retain recognizable tidal phasing, but component biases show a different distribution of horizontal shear. At the surface, Vx bias is +0.068 m s⁻¹ and Vy bias is −0.158 m s⁻¹. Vz magnitudes are small in both models and are not used as the main diagnostic.

4. Middle-estuary results

The middle location shows the clearest transition from relatively good free-surface agreement to substantial baroclinic disagreement.

Water surface

Water-level RMSE is 0.0374 m, with a +0.0350 m SCHISM bias and correlation of 0.992.

Thermohaline structure

TUFLOW FV progressively develops a strong vertical gradient, with high near-bottom salinity and temperature retained while the surface remains fresher and cooler. SCHISM becomes comparatively fresh and cool at all sampled depths. Near-bottom salinity RMSE is 25.72 psu; near-bottom temperature RMSE is 5.48 °C.

Velocity

Mid-depth Vx and Vy timing remains strongly correlated (R = 0.937 and 0.912), but surface component biases remain evident. This indicates that tidal timing alone does not explain the thermohaline divergence; the vertical distribution of transport is also different.

5. Upper-estuary results

At the upper location, the models agree comparatively well at the surface after the initial freshening, but differ strongly at depth.

Water surface

Water-level RMSE is 0.0395 m, bias is +0.0349 m and correlation is 0.984.

Surface versus lower layer

Surface salinity RMSE is only 1.55 psu and surface temperature RMSE is 1.19 °C. In contrast, TUFLOW FV develops a saline and warm lower layer while SCHISM remains fresh and cold. Near-bottom salinity and temperature RMSE reach 22.52 psu and 8.43 °C, respectively.

Implication

The agreement at the surface but large discrepancy at depth points to a difference in lower-layer intrusion and vertical exchange, rather than simply a uniform offset in the imposed temperature or salinity fields.

Mean surface velocity is −0.16478 m s⁻¹ (outward), while mid-depth and near-bottom means are +0.01719 and +0.01657 m s⁻¹ (inward).

SCHISM: outward residual at all levels

Mean velocities are −0.05982, −0.04813 and −0.01954 m s⁻¹ from surface to near-bottom.

TUFLOW FV retains stronger salinity stratification

Mean salinity increases from 23.11 psu at the surface to 33.60 psu near the bed. SCHISM ranges only from 29.68 to 30.53 psu.

Salt-advection sign differs at depth

TUFLOW FV is negative at the surface and positive at mid-depth and near-bottom. SCHISM remains negative at all three levels.

8. Key findings

  1. The exact horizontal mesh match removes horizontal geometry as the primary explanation.
  2. Water-level timing and amplitude are broadly consistent, although SCHISM has a small positive elevation bias in the middle and upper estuary.
  3. The dominant discrepancy is baroclinic. TUFLOW FV develops a vertically stratified estuary and a saline, warm lower-layer intrusion; SCHISM does not reproduce the same structure over the six-day benchmark.
  4. The ocean residual diagnostic is consistent with the point results. TUFLOW FV shows outward surface transport and inward transport at depth, while SCHISM has outward mean transport at all sampled levels.
