Executive summary

The two models reproduce the same estuary geometry and several common first-order longitudinal patterns. Their vertical behaviour is not yet equivalent: TUFLOW FV-AED resolves pronounced depth structure, while the supplied SCHISM-AED water-quality outputs are frequently close to uniform over the active layers. SCHISM-AED PAR and total-light diagnostics are effectively zero and require configuration checking before biological differences are interpreted.

2. Dissolved oxygen

The horizontal maps share the same broad downstream decline. The curtains differ strongly: TUFLOW resolves a high-oxygen surface layer and lower oxygen at depth, while SCHISM-AED is nearly uniform vertically at each chainage. Because the SCHISM grid contains multiple active layers, the result should be investigated as a coupling, mixing, initialization or extraction issue—not described as a one-layer mesh.

3. Velocity, TSS and total phosphorus

TUFLOW shows visible vertical differences, particularly in velocity and TSS. The supplied SCHISM result is more uniform through the active layers and shows stronger downstream TSS and TP increases. The exact SCHISM snapshot for this composite should be confirmed before quantitative interpretation.

4. P2 TSS profile and time series

TUFLOW shows a vertical concentration gradient and gradual accumulation over the run. SCHISM is almost depth-uniform at the matched profile time and later produces large event-like pulses exceeding 9–10 mg/L.

5. P1 Hovmöller diagrams

TUFLOW contains persistent depth dependence. SCHISM displays nearly vertical colour bands, indicating simultaneous water-column changes and little independent depth response.

6. Gross primary production

Both fields are near the bottom of their plotted ranges, but the statistic, vertical extraction and units are not fully harmonised. SCHISM GPP should be reassessed together with its near-zero light diagnostics.

7. Nutrients, chlorophyll-a and oxygen

Each variable is presented as a full-width TUFLOW figure followed by the corresponding full-width SCHISM figure. This avoids shrinking long curtain plots.

TUFLOW shows stronger vertical organisation. SCHISM TN and chlorophyll-a are dominated by the downstream transition and by synchronized late-period pulses shared with TSS and TP. This remains a qualitative comparison because the displayed timestamps differ by 24 hours and units are not fully aligned.

8. Light environment

Long curtain plots are presented one per row. Model-specific density, phytoplankton and GPP context is also kept at full width.

TUFLOW PAR and total light attenuate with depth. The SCHISM fields are effectively zero, making this the highest-priority configuration check. Verify radiation forcing, optical parameters, AED variable registration and interface units.

10. Findings and recommended next steps

  1. Confirm initial, boundary and source concentrations and units.
  2. Verify radiation forcing and optical coupling in SCHISM-AED.
  3. Export vertical coordinates and layer thicknesses with the water-quality fields.
  4. Apply one shared plotting specification for both models.
  5. Generate station and curtain CSVs for automated difference metrics.
  6. Repeat the six-day case after light and vertical-structure checks.

Conclusion. The common diagnostic workflow is established, but the present outputs are not yet a like-for-like three-dimensional water-quality comparison. The differences are specific and testable, providing a clear route to formal inter-model verification.
